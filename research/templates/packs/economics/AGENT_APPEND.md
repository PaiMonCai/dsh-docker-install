
## Economics research rules

This project uses the Economics Research Pack.

- Start by stating the economic question, target population, unit of observation, outcome, treatment/exposure, and estimand.
- Do not call a coefficient causal unless the identification strategy and assumptions support a causal interpretation.
- For panel/fixed-effects work, record entity/time fixed effects, the variation identifying the coefficient, and the clustering level.
- For Difference-in-Differences or event studies, record treatment timing, comparison group, event-time normalization, anticipation assumptions, and pre-trend evidence. Do not treat a non-significant pre-trend test as proof of parallel trends.
- Before estimating DiD, run `research-econ-did check` to detect duplicate unit-time rows, inconsistent cohorts, treatment reversals, and cohort/treatment mismatches.
- For staggered adoption, TWFE may be kept as a baseline, but do not rely on it alone when treatment effects may be heterogeneous; compare DID2S and/or saturated cohort-interacted event-study estimates when appropriate.
- Prefer `research-run --name <label> -- research-econ-did estimate ...` for paper-producing DiD results so the data hash, Git state, timing diagnostics, estimates, figures, and warnings remain traceable.
- Pointwise lead p-values are diagnostics, not proof for or against parallel trends; interpret pre-treatment evidence jointly with design knowledge and appropriate joint/simultaneous inference.
- For IV/2SLS, record the instrument, first stage, exclusion restriction, relevance evidence, and the population represented by the estimand.
- For RDD, record the running variable, cutoff, bandwidth choice, functional form, manipulation checks, and continuity assumptions.
- Distinguish nominal and real values, units, deflators, base years, logs, growth rates, percentages, and percentage points.
- State exactly how standard errors are computed and clustered. Do not silently use default standard errors when the design calls for clustering or other dependence corrections.
- Preserve the full sample construction path: exclusions, merges, missing-value handling, winsorization/trimming, and outlier rules.
- Keep the baseline specification defined before inspecting a large specification search. Do not select controls, fixed effects, bandwidths, or subsamples merely because they improve significance.
- Report economically meaningful effect sizes and uncertainty, not only p-values or stars.
- Robustness, heterogeneity, mechanism, placebo, and falsification results must be generated by code and unsuccessful checks must not be hidden.
- For paper-producing linear/fixed-effects regressions, prefer `research-run --name <label> -- research-econ-model feols ...` so estimation, output hashes, logs, and the paper include are all traceable.
- `research-econ-model` requires an explicit variance estimator; do not omit `--vcov` and silently accept package defaults.
- Do not hand-edit `paper/generated/economics-results.qmd`, generated regression tables, or generated coefficient figures. Regenerate them from model manifests instead.
- For a publication-style main-results table, register each specification separately and use `research-econ-model compare`; do not manually copy coefficients across models.
- When using external macro/financial databases, record provider, series ID, vintage/access date, frequency, units, seasonal adjustment, and transformations.
- Prefer `research-econ-data worldbank ...` or `research-econ-data fred ...` for supported external data so the raw snapshot, retrieval metadata, and SHA256 are recorded automatically.
- Treat `data/raw/external/` snapshots as immutable. Transform them into `data/processed/` rather than editing downloaded files in place.
